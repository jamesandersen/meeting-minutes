## 2. Technical Discussions & Working Group Consensus

- **Topic:** Mandate and deliverable timeline
  - **Discussion:** The group was chartered to move [RFC #550](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/550) from a proposal to a formal specification. The mandate is to align on the problem and scope, make decisions in the open, involve the wider community, and deliver the results to the Shopping Tech Council as pull requests.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus to wrap up the bulk product discovery work by late October 2026, aligning with the November/December release cycle.

- **Topic:** Operating model — asynchronous decision-making
  - **Discussion:** Because participants are spread across many time zones, and no single session can hold all of them, live debate cannot be the decision mechanism. Decisions will be tracked in a position log in the DWG working document, where members record recommendations, objections, and rationale. Where no substantive objection is raised, a recommendation proceeds; where there is conflict, an optional deep-dive session is scheduled to resolve it. Approved decisions are surfaced to the community as pull requests.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus to adopt asynchronous decision-making as the default for all decisions.

- **Topic:** Publication of meeting artifacts
  - **Discussion:** The group considered what to publish from its sessions.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus that raw transcripts and raw recordings will not be published. Only sanitized minutes are released externally. _(Atlantic session)_

- **Topic:** Scope strategy — "crawl, walk, run"
  - **Discussion:** The problem space is broad enough that attempting to solve every edge case at once would stall the group. Ilya Grigorik argued for first defining the base shape of a feed and drawing explicit boundaries around what that base feed covers, deferring harder cases to later phases. The Pacific session framed the same idea as a "minimum viable kernel," with non-essential items such as campaigns and advanced collections moved to a parking-lot section of the working document rather than debated now. Participants used the thought experiment of standing up a new search engine to identify the bare-bones requirements for a first version.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus on the crawl/walk/run approach and on parking non-essential scope. Final P0 versus P1 assignment was **deferred** — see section 3.

- **Topic:** Mapping the discussion onto the existing decision framework
  - **Discussion:** The group reviewed the decision categories already drafted in the working document — wire format, extensibility, sync mechanisms, access and authentication, source authority — and checked that the live discussion mapped onto them. James Andersen observed that many of the proposed decisions already touch on the items being raised, but that the group welcomes the initial divergent thinking rather than steering discussion straight into the existing categories, so that the problem space is properly absorbed before scope is narrowed. Convergence onto the decision framework is expected to follow.

- **Topic:** Problem statement — ingestion scalability and cost
  - **Discussion:** Vinod Kumar described crawling as the common fallback for product discovery and an expensive, capacity-intensive one. Unauthenticated public APIs from storefront builders do return structured data, but the absence of a standard forces bespoke per-source integrations and significant operational overhead. The goal is to encourage businesses that already hold this data to expose it in a standardized bulk format.

- **Topic:** Problem statement — product identity mismatch
  - **Discussion:** Ilya Grigorik framed this as an ecosystem problem rather than a UCP technical failure. When feeds pass through intermediaries, those intermediaries often mint their own fabricated product IDs. Agents that later attempt a live cart call or price check against the merchant fail, because the merchant does not recognize the ID. Current UCP catalog endpoints return null for identifiers created by third-party aggregators. The ecosystem needs to prioritize passing through authoritative merchant IDs.

- **Topic:** Problem statement — dynamic merchandising and context
  - **Discussion:** Pricing and availability vary by geography, user status, and campaign rules. Ilya Grigorik noted it is frequently impossible to materialize every variant of a feed given how dynamic these configurations are. Alex Jansen added that regional shipping costs and zip-code-level local pricing make a basic feed insufficient without layering further specification on top. Merchants commonly resolve these through dynamic engines at request time.

- **Topic:** Relationship between bulk feeds and real-time APIs
  - **Discussion:** Jason Mathnov raised that bulk feeds carry latency that makes them unable to power real-time experiences on their own, and that a pathway to query real-time price and availability must be preserved so ads and checkout stay accurate. The Pacific session reached the same conclusion from the constraints side: a universal real-time feed is not physically achievable for every use case.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus that a feed is a point-in-time snapshot of a database serving discovery, and that it must be combined with real-time APIs for authoritative "last mile" transactions. Feeds are explicitly not intended to solve every problem alone.

- **Topic:** Separating feed discovery from the feed data model
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen proposed treating these as two distinct problems: how a consumer finds a feed, and what the feed itself contains. Ilya Grigorik supported this and noted discovery could be simplified by reusing the existing UCP merchant profile — adding a feed capability to the merchant's well-known profile to advertise where the feed is hosted. _(Atlantic session)_

- **Topic:** Reusing existing UCP primitives
  - **Discussion:** Rather than reinventing standards, the group discussed building on existing UCP catalog primitives. Ilya Grigorik explained that the UCP profile and context objects already support filtering and user-specific parameters, which could be transposed onto feed definitions. Participants were encouraged to experiment with the UCP CLI to see how live queries and filters currently behave, since that structure may already be sufficient to express feed requirements.

- **Topic:** Wire format and serialization
  - **Discussion:** The group favored industry-standard recommendations over mandating a single format. Ilya Grigorik observed that JSONL suits small merchants while Parquet is more sensible for aggregated catalogs. The Pacific session independently landed on remaining file-format agnostic for the initial version. _(Atlantic session raised the specific formats)_
  - **Working Group Position:** Define general rules and publish recommendations rather than mandate one format.

- **Topic:** Merchant control and feed flexibility
  - **Discussion:** Vinod Kumar advocated giving merchants flexibility to expose feeds scoped by market, currency, or language without the protocol imposing restrictions, with consumers applying filters to manage the data. The group affirmed that the protocol should enable business policies — what a merchant shares and with whom — rather than dictate them.

- **Topic:** Risk of ecosystem fragmentation
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen cautioned against creating another siloed standard, noting that no merchants were represented in the room and that feed changes are costly for merchants to implement. Ilya Grigorik countered that the participating organizations collectively represent a very large number of merchants, and that the aim is a coherent ingestion path for a new class of agent-based discovery experiences rather than a replacement for widely adopted existing formats.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus to introduce UCP as an additional standard, not to mandate convergence onto it or supplant existing formats such as those already in use at Google. The group agreed to document this concern and the associated risks as the scope is refined. _(Atlantic session)_

- **Topic:** Meeting cadence and schedule
  - **Discussion:** Weekly 45-minute meetings, with deep dives scheduled as needed and agendas published 24 hours in advance per the UCP communication guidelines. The Pacific session agreed to shift the recurring slot by one hour to improve attendance, and the group discussed a rotating or split schedule for future sessions.
